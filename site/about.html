What is Fashion Lab

Re-imagine what you already own

Fashion Lab Sedona is a community sustainable fashion event. Each fall, local designers spend a 48-hour Create-a-Thon turning thrift-store finds, discarded clothing and reclaimed fabric into new looks, which walk the runway a week later alongside Sensational Sedonans dressed by local boutique, The Closet Concierge.

The fashion industry produces about 92 million tons of textile waste a year, and less than 20% of textiles are reused or recycled. We answer with small-batch, local creativity that brings the community together, one stitch at a time. Fashion Lab Sedona is a project of LightNet, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, and the Dream Machine, bringing communities together in joy. Founder and producer: Zenka Caro.

Year one · 2024

Where it all started

Fashion Lab Sedona began in 2024 with a core team of volunteers, a borrowed studio and a budget under $10,000.

The idea was to unite the Sedona community over fashion. It was modeled on 48-hour create-a-thons, where filmmakers, programmers and creatives collaborate over one dynamic weekend, and it was born as part of the Dream Machine collaboration, a prototype other cities could replicate.

That first season brought the very first 48 Hour Create-a-Thon, the first runway show and wrap party in August, and the first pop-up workshops: bleach dye, stencils and a backyard full of t-shirts. The Verde Independent covered it as Sedona is in Stitches.

Year two · 2025

Stitching a sustainable future

In year two, Nathan Quinn developed the Fashion Lab funding model after a vision: a virtuous cycle where every contributor is also a benefactor, and every group that benefits from the show chips in a little.

Julia Rotella helped set up a nine-member board to guide decisions together, and Nathan produced a full-scale fashion show. The Create-a-Thon ran November 7 to 9 at Shelley Evans' Ultimate Light Mission, backed by a $5,000 City of Sedona grant. Mary, Jesselynn and Stephen sparked creativity all year with free pop-up workshops: How 2 Model, Swap & Stitch, costume design, and fairy vests, upcycled boots and hats with Sherri Columbus. 2025 also launched the first Sustainable Fashion Award, a $5,000 package of $1,000 cash and a $4,000 FABRIC Tempe scholarship. Rachel Weir took it home.

Year three · 2026

A sold-out third show

Twenty designers, twelve Sensational Sedonans and about 200 guests at Brady Hall, with more than 40 Sedona organizations rallying around the show.

The Create-a-Thon ran October 2 to 4 at Ultimate Light Mission, and the looks walked the runway on October 10. Jesselynn Desmond won the $5,000 Sustainable Design Award with FABRIC Incubator, with Inok Alrutz and Hollie Laban close behind. Sedona Dance Academy joined the show, and the show ran without a city grant: tickets and the bar made up the gap. We also wrote the whole thing down as a free playbook so other towns can run their own Fashion Lab.
