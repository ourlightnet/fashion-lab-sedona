Why a template

Your town already has everything it needs

A thrift store. A few people who sew. A hall with a long aisle. Twelve people everybody knows. That is a Fashion Lab.

Fashion Lab Sedona started in 2024 with a core team of volunteers, a borrowed studio and a budget under $10,000. By 2026 it was a sold-out show with twenty designers, sponsors, vendors, a raffle, a $5,000 design award and an after party. The format pays for itself, builds a real creative community, and keeps clothes out of the landfill.

This page is the whole playbook. Use it for free. If you want us in the room, or on a call, consulting is available on site or remotely for a fee.

01 · The money model

Who pays what, and why it works

Nathan Quinn developed this plan in year 2 after a vision. It is a virtuous cycle: every contributor is also a benefactor. Every group that benefits from the show chips in a little. These are Sedona's 2026 numbers.

12 local celebrity models$200 each

"Sensational Sedonans": well-known locals who buy a full-page program ad, walk the runway in 2–3 outfit changes and bring their whole crowd to the show.

3 sustainable boutiques$1,500 each

Thrift, consignment, conscious or fair-trade shops pay to dress the Sensational Sedonans. Their clothes get the runway, their name gets the room.

Designers$100 each

Registration for the 48 Hour Create-a-Thon. Includes snacks, show filming, a program profile and a website bio. Twenty designers in 2026.

Vendor tables$200 each

A 3' × 6' table in a prime spot during the VIP reception and after the show, two tickets, logo on the site and in the program.

Program ads$50 – $250

Quarter page to full-page cover ads in the printed and digital show program. Easy yes for any local business.

Underwriters & retail sponsors$1,000 – $5,000

"Fashion Lab [Your Town] presented by…". Logo everywhere for a year, VIP runway seating, a round table at the show.

Tickets$30 – $100

Early bird two months out, general admission, and VIP with the pre-show reception. Paper tickets sold by hand work surprisingly well.

Donors & the big raffleIn-kind + raffle

Ten to twelve high-end raffle items donated by local businesses. The raffle runs the night of the show while the judges are deciding.

City grantUp to $5,000

Most cities have a small arts or community grant. Sedona's covered the first year's venue. Apply early.

Where it goes

A scholarship and prizes: $1,750 cash from the Lab plus an $8,000 course-and-mentorship package donated by the FABRIC Incubator in Tempe. A venue. Pipe and drape. Food for 48 hours. A videographer. Printing. The rest rolls into next year.

02 · The core team

Eight people, eight roles

Volunteers burn out when everyone does everything. Give each person one lane. Everyone recruits their own crew.

01

Producer

  • Holds the vision, the budget and the calendar
  • Grants, underwriters, the city, the fiscal sponsor
  • Signs the contracts
02

Creative Director & Runway Producer

  • Show line-up, rehearsal, music, MC script
  • How-to-model classes and model call
  • Stage manager, hair and makeup crew
03

Create-a-Thon Lead

  • Designer registration and what to bring
  • Fabric and clothing drive, food, set-up and tear-down crews
  • Judging hand-in on Sunday
04

Sensational Sedonans & Boutique Lead

  • Recruits the 12 local celebrities
  • Signs the 3 boutiques, schedules fittings
  • Dressers for VIPs on show day
05

Website & Program Lead

  • Website updates, posters, flyers, tickets, signage
  • Program layout and ad specs
  • Sponsor logos and the thank-you graphic
06

Social Media & Press Lead

  • Monthly pop-up posts, scheduling tool
  • Press blurbs to the local paper and radio
  • Hashtags, reels, photographer and videographer
07

Pop-Ups & Workshops Lead

  • One free or low-cost workshop a month
  • Finds the host, the space, the teacher
  • Turns attendees into volunteers and designers
08

Volunteers, Raffle & Logistics Lead

  • Venue, insurance, parking, tables, linens
  • Reception desk, lanyards, volunteer pre-meeting
  • Raffle items and the raffle on show night

Sedona's full role sheet also lists: MC ×2, stage manager, makeup, hair, printed program lead, raffle lead, photographer, look photographer, videographer, lead volunteer, reception ×4, DJ, set-up crew ×3, dressers ×2, clean-up crew ×4, sewing assistance ×3, and recruiting leads for the neighbouring towns.

03 · The season

Six months, one weekend, one night

Sedona runs May to October. Pick your show date first and count back.

  1. 6 months out

    Lock the frame

    • Find the venue, set the show date and the Create-a-Thon weekend (one week before)
    • Update the contracts, create the budget, apply for the city grant
    • Set up ticketing, recruit the core team of 8
  2. 5 – 4 months out

    Pop-ups start

    • Movie night, sewing 101, bleach or natural dye, clothing swap
    • Sign the underwriter and the 3 boutiques
    • Website up, Instagram alive
  3. 3 months out

    Open the doors

    • Designer registration opens ($100, first come first served)
    • Recruit the 12 Sensational Sedonans, sell program ads and vendor tables
    • Print business cards, flyers, posters
  4. 2 months out

    Sell

    • Early-bird tickets on sale, paper tickets in hand
    • Model call, how-to-model classes
    • Press release, raffle items confirmed
  5. Show week −1

    48 Hour Create-a-Thon

    • Friday 6pm to Sunday 6pm
    • Designs and descriptions turned in Sunday 3pm for judging
    • Sensational Sedonan fittings at the boutiques
  6. Show day

    The Fashion Show

    • 11am rehearsal · 1pm hair & makeup · 4pm VIP doors · 5pm show · 7pm vendor sales · 8pm out
    • Raffle while the judges decide, awards, after party
  7. After

    Close the loop

    • Photos and videos out within two weeks, thank-you graphic to every sponsor
    • Report to the city, "do the same / do differently" meeting
    • Book next year's venue

07 · Lessons from Sedona

Do the same. Do differently.

Straight from our post-show meeting. Steal these before you learn them the hard way.

Do the same
  1. Keep designer registration at $100. It filters for commitment.
  2. Paper tickets, distributed and sold ahead by the core team.
  3. Sensational Sedonans, vendor tables and program ads: the three easy revenue lines.
  4. The music was great. Keep the same DJ.
Do differently · money
  1. Early-bird pricing two months ahead ($30), $45 after, $60 at the door, $90 VIP. General tickets should be higher than $30.
  2. Limit the raffle to 10 – 12 high-end items, invite only.
  3. Give the boutiques more selling time before the show.
  4. Have a designated sales person after the show. People wanted to buy the outfits.
Do differently · the show
  1. Write the show structure down ahead of time with the key people. One host for the whole night, an MC for the runway segment.
  2. Rehearse the day before, not only the morning of.
  3. A floor host to guide VIPs and general ticket holders in separately.
  4. More light on the runway. Photographers struggled with pools of light.
  5. Stronger backstage communication; a runner between the dressing room and the floor.
  6. Separate dressing rooms.
Do differently · Create-a-Thon
  1. Outfits for the competition must be made inside the 48 hours.
  2. Descriptions turned in on Sunday, the last day.
  3. A quiet room for the video interviews.
  4. Tell designers what to bring: scissors, tape measure, pins, chalk, seam ripper, paper and pencil, their own machine if they have one. Before-and-after photos of every piece.
  5. More ironing boards.
  6. One person in charge of volunteers, with a pre-meeting a few days ahead and lanyards on the night.
2026 debrief

The audience wants to see the work

From Mary Dancer Browne's 2026 debrief. The Create-a-Thon felt magical; the biggest lesson from the show was giving the designers' creations the time and attention they deserve.

Create-a-Thon
  1. Eat lunch together. It built the community more than anything else.
  2. Set a real lunch break, so designers don't feel torn between eating, talking and sewing.
  3. Favorites: a big pot of vegetable soup, pizza and sandwiches, brownies, barbecue chips, popcorn. Add fresh fruit; buy less of everything else.
  4. Keep a shared volunteer list, and name one volunteer coordinator.
  5. Keep the same venue year to year. It becomes the event's home.
  6. Text designers early and one to one for anything important. Group threads get muted.
The runway
  1. Rehearse the day before the show. It doesn't have to be at the venue.
  2. Hold the "how to model" workshops at easy times, never during the Create-a-Thon.
  3. Give the judged designer looks at least as much runway time as sponsor looks.
  4. Walk slower, with pauses and pivots, and pick music that doesn't make models rush.
  5. Use the whole runway, including the cross aisle, so every seat sees the outfits.
  6. Share the story behind each design.
The room
  1. Protect the runway camera: a sign and a volunteer beside it.
  2. Nobody crosses the runway. Mark a walking route behind the audience and the camera.
  3. More greeters: welcome guests, walk VIPs to the lounge, point to restrooms, explain the layout.
  4. Give volunteers a short run-of-show briefing: timing, who runs what, seating, runway rules, camera spots, intermission, emergencies.
Guests and designers
  1. Use intermission to let guests meet designers and see the garments up close. Plan a moment for the second half too.
  2. Simplify the raffle: a silent auction, winners drawn during intermission and posted in the lobby.
  3. Let designers' models use hair and makeup artists who are free. Cosmetology students were a great addition.
  4. Open the step-and-repeat to every guest.
  5. Tell the audience how to join next year: signs, announcements, the website and the mailing list.
  6. Record short testimonials right after the show, while the energy is high, with permission.
